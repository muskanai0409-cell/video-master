# Lane C: her own library, mined for what the films have not used yet (2026-10-15)

Contract: `00_contract.md`. Question for this lane: inside everything Muskan has bookmarked, sent, saved or studied, which techniques have the films never used, what makes motion look expensive, and which references define her taste?

**What "new" means here.** A technique counts as new when it is not in `video-master/references/motion-grammar.md` or `motion-systems.md`. Where something already exists elsewhere (a recipe, the lessons log section 3, an engine folder), the row says so.

**Refs.** `3#24` is batch 3, entry 24, in `video-master/references/library/videos/3-code-craft.md`. `12f#4` is entry 4 of `12-more-f.md`. `13#1` is entry 1 of `13-shared-oct.md`. Each row links the post. For quoted clips the link is the quoting post.

**Tags**
- `[brand ok]`: fits the supermemory site surface or brand book as it stands.
- `[off-brand]`: banned on the supermemory surface today (gradients, glow, 3D, shadows, particles, glass). Listed for other films, or for a brief that asks for a new register.
- `[bans]`: clashes with the banned list in motion-grammar §6. Listed because it is in her library; use only if she lifts the ban.
- `[footage]`: needs a camera plate or generated video.
- `[verify]`: from a source that could not be read in full.

**Sources read:** the whole library folder (websites, bookmarks, shared links, video study, all 13 batch files covering 304 studied videos plus the October batch), the recipes, inspiration, gallery lessons, her four Instagram notes, the lessons log (#1 to #99) and design tells; then live pages, read only (section 5).

## 0. The short version

- **Her library holds far more range than the films use.** 163 techniques below are in her saves and in neither motion file. The biggest untouched groups are type (30), UI motion (24) and transitions (23).
- **The "watermark" is mostly type and endings.** Every statement rises from a mask line in Geist with ink-45% dimming, and nearly every film ends object, then mark, then horizontal lockup, then a still (section 4.1). Her library has 30 other type treatments (2.1) and several other endings (C63, C115, C118).
- **Premium, in her own sources:** one object, one accent with a meaning, light and focus doing the editing instead of cuts, serif statements on cream or paper, colour held back until the payoff, two cadences (type steps, the world glides), a real shutter, and calm critically damped physics (section 1).
- **The biggest gap is serif and depth.** All six readers of the batch files flagged depth, and four of the six flagged the missing serif. The engines hold only Geist and Geist Mono, and have no depth stack, focus pull or perspective camera (section 4.2).
- **Template alarm.** Border beams, word-by-word blur-ins, scrambled headings, aurora blobs, logo marquees, orbiting-icon hubs, count-ups from zero and glass or chrome each ship in 5 to 8 of the 10 component libraries she saved. That is why they read as AI-made (section 1.3).
- **@ascenta.studio sells animated product heroes.** Its concepts (perfume, lipstick, a bag, a watch) are one product render under one dramatic light, in rich materials, turning slowly: the premium register in row 26.

### 4.2 What her library lacks (look elsewhere)

1. **Editorial serif type.** Four of the six batch readers flagged it. The premium films set statements in serif, but the engines hold only Geist and Geist Mono, and no saved reference teaches serif motion (line-masked reveals, optical sizes, tracking). Look at type-foundry specimen films and editorial titles.
2. **Depth and optics.** No depth stack, focus pull, perspective camera, turntable or raymarched material in the engines (`engine/gpu` is new and holds no UI). Her saves show the moves (C138 to C145) but not how studios light and grade them.
3. **Sound.** She has never sent a sound or music link (shared-links §F), and the study measured loudness only. The Don Toliver lane remains unmet after six asks. There is no reference for sound design that follows the picture.
4. **Style anchors outside tech.** The library is almost all SaaS launch films. Gallery lesson #9 says to name an anchor; her saves contain only Bauhaus, an Apple keynote and a Broadway trailer as examples. There are no print, poster, editorial, title-sequence or broadcast references.
5. **Loop-native formats.** Her films end on a held lockup; the library has loops (C81, C82, C115) but no engine checks loop seams in position and velocity (the howseen QA does).
6. **Graph and footage tooling.** Five-plus graph moves (C77, C78, C97, C102) with no force-graph engine; footage devices (C21, C95, C153) need a matting pass the engines lack.
7. **Premium web, from the inside.** Her saves show the look (Ascenta's product heroes, the Renaissance-painting site by @kryntixstudio, @w.wearebrand's build) but no studio explains its choices. Current Awwwards Sites of the Day include work by Active Theory (Santioni Spirits, Oct 5), Locomotive (L.I.S.A., Sep 16) and Riotters (Lidar Drone Scanning, Oct 6); their case studies are where to look. landing.love's own tags say the animation-heavy web is mostly GSAP (191 sites), WebGL (134) and Three.js (74), and 570 sites are tagged 3D, which reinforces gap 2.
8. **Colour grading.** Apart from the learner-1 film's act-break grade, nothing in the library teaches grading.
9. **The @0xwhrrari "Motion Engineering" article.** Both @rossaxbt templates quote it; it needs her X login and is still unread.
