# Recipe: ad

A short brand piece that borrows a genre or tells a small story, with the product as the payoff rather than the subject. It runs 15 to 140s. 8 of 156 in the set.

**Use it when** the goal is attention and a feeling (a teaser, a stunt, a cultural moment), not a demo.

**On supermemory** her brand rules still apply. Never imitate a real brand's product, name or trade dress, even as parody.

## Targets (8 ads, medians)

| Measure | Median | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 45s | 15 to 25s for a teaser; 60 to 140s only with a story |
| Moving | 77% | 75%+ |
| Longest still | 0.9s | under 1s |
| Cuts per minute | 15.1 | 15 to 40, with a fixed anchor when fast (see below) |
| Median shot | 3.0s | 1 to 3s |
| Loudness | -17.5 LUFS | -14 |

## Shapes that worked

| Shape | Example | How |
|---|---|---|
| Transplant a genre | 1#7, 2#8, 2#15 | Take a genre's stock signals (a trailer's letterbox and dusk grade; a premium phone ad's macro glides and price card; a press conference and a jersey reveal) and fill them with your message. The mismatch is the hook. End on a date or a two-card punchline |
| Product as status lines over a human story | 1#16 | The product appears only as a growing stack of finished-task chips over footage, so "it works while you're away" is countable |
| Product beside the subject | 2#5, 5#11, 3#32 | Glass panels or voice pills float in the real room and update as the story moves. No screen recording |
| One accent across unrelated images | 2#4 | An orange dot marks where to look in every shot, so about 20 images in 14s read as one thought. A word ladder (ask, task, craft) carries the message, and it loops to frame 0 |
| Trailer of proof | 6#21 | News stakes, then proof as giant one-line numbers, then a notification as the outcome, then the logo |

## Rules

- **Fast cuts need one fixed anchor:** a dot, an arc or a line at fixed coordinates (2#4, 1#19).
- **Prove personalisation with one callback** to something the user said earlier ("I know you like visuals", 5#11).
- **One sound-making action per shot,** as the beat rule (3#32).
- **Split the punchline over two short end cards** (2#8).

## Pitfalls

- Parodying a real product or trade dress (2#8 is labelled parody; don't follow it).
- Celebrity or likeness use without rights.
- Money and get-paid claims (6#21).
- Mastering loud for its own sake.
