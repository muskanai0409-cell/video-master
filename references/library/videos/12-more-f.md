# Batch 12-more-f: podcasts, interview clips, a tutorial and a founder film (6 entries)

> **Method note.** Frames come from exact-seek 16-frame sheets (one frame at (k+0.5)·T/16, with T capped at 240s, so for the long videos the sheet covers only the first 240s), 6-frame hook strips (0-2.5s) and 6-frame end strips (the last 4s). For the three long videos (#2 at 85 min, #3 at 37 min, #5 at 102 min) I also pulled 12 evenly spaced frames across the full runtime, one frame per shot through each cold open, and frames across the closing minutes. For #1, #4 and #6 I pulled extra frames around the opening cut, the transitions and each shot. Audio was not heard; only its presence and loudness are reported. Views are the bookmarked post's, so the two quoted clips (#5, #6) show the quoting post's views. Times are approximate (±1s). The cut detector missed some hard cuts between similar-toned shots and every whip or wipe (#1, #3, #4), so low cut counts are lower bounds.

## Batch patterns

- **Formats (6):** 4 talking heads (the Zuckerberg interview clip, the Invest Like the Best and Distribution episodes, the a16z clip), 1 tutorial (Riley Brown's GrokBot guide) and 1 launch film (The Prompting Company). Three are long-form (37, 85 and 102 minutes) and three run 25-82s. No competitor videos.
- **Topic thread:** four of the six are about personal agents that know your context (a personal AI that knows your life, Instinct's assistant, GrokBot's team of agents, proactive suggestions from context); the other two are about how agents choose products and how a company grew.
- **Reach:** the top post (6.36M) is the least produced: 25s, one shot after 0.45s, no type at all, posted by a third-party account that put the claim and a question in the text. The 85-minute episode (1.79M) comes from a large account. Both quote posts drew far less than the posts they quote (13K vs 314K, 3.3K vs 310K).
- **Saves vs reach:** the viral clip has the lowest save rate (0.03% of views), the 85-minute episode sits at 0.2% and the 37-minute tutorial is highest (1.1%): clips buy reach, chaptered long-form gets saved.
- **Cold opens:** both full episodes open with a teaser before any title. Invest Like the Best: 8 un-captioned shots in 36s, then a 14s black-and-white title (sponsor, guest name, host name, show logo). Distribution: 50s at ~1.2s a shot with giant serif keywords and on-screen receipts (posts, a TV clip, a press paragraph), name supers at 13s. Riley's tutorial does the same job in 43s with kinetic type, a collage of other people's posts, product footage and a follower badge.
- **Body grammar:** two or three locked-off cameras (a wide plus singles), with the guest or main speaker in roughly 60-75% of samples. Cutting collapses after the open: Distribution makes 32 of its first 38 cuts in 50s, Invest Like the Best 12 of 32. The a16z clip breaks one speaker with two 1.5s reactions and a 12s wide.
- **Branding and type:** a small constant corner mark (Distribution's wordmark, the a16z logo, Invest Like the Best's logo plus a sponsor row); the Zuckerberg clip has none. Only Distribution and The Prompting Company put the spoken argument on screen as giant words, so the other four give a muted viewer little beyond faces.
- **Endings:** none of the six ends on a call to action. Invest Like the Best ends on ~48s of full-screen sponsor cards (~12s each), Distribution fades to black, Riley stops on his written tip list, and the clips stop mid-thought. Only The Prompting Company has a designed end card (an edited motto, then the wordmark, held ~2s).
- **Loudness:** the dialogue-led long-form sits at -19 to -24 LUFS; the three short pieces at -15 to -16.
- **Detector caveat:** the 0.32 threshold missed #1's 0.45s cut, #4's cuts at ~7s and ~15.7s and #3's whips and wipes, so treat low counts as lower bounds.

**Lessons for her videos:**
1. Clip one complete answer of 25-80s, open on a sub-second listener shot, break it only with a short reaction or one wide, and write the claim plus a question into the post: the Zuckerberg clip reached 6.36M with no on-screen type. On a smaller account, add one or two giant keywords per line as Distribution does, because a bare clip gives a muted viewer nothing.
2. Give every long episode a 35-50s receipts cold open before the title: the best lines at ~1s a shot, one or two giant words per bite, and each claim followed within a second by its proof (a post, a chart, a broadcast); then name supers by ~15s and a calm three-camera body.
3. Start the show with a title sequence in a different look (Invest Like the Best drops to black and white and names guest, host and show over 14s), keep one small corner mark for the whole runtime, and end on a designed card rather than a cut to black.
4. For long tutorials, sell before teaching: the promise as kinetic type by ~9s, proof from other people's posts, the product moving, then one fixed screen-plus-PiP layout chaptered in the post, ending on a single recap page of every tip.
5. For a team or founder film, relay one argument across speakers with the sentence building across the cuts, matte the biggest words behind the speaker, and close by striking one word of a familiar line and typing the new one.
