**Lessons on AI footage under code-drawn type:**
1. Use AI footage for what code can't draw (people, light, weather, water, crowds). Keep everything that must be exact (UI, numbers, charts, text, logos) code-drawn; none of these 24 trusts AI with on-screen text.
2. Keep AI shots to 1.2-3s and cut on motion. Use one long take (8-15s) only when realism itself is the claim (#12's opener, #16, #20).
3. Lock every plate to one light and one style lens: one time of day, one period look, one recurring character or object. That way a montage reads as one world (#1, #9, #11).
4. Generate plates with empty space kept for type, and set the labels there (#18 splits words around the subject; #4 puts pills on the empty side).
5. Bridge footage and UI with in-world carriers (a prompt bar over the scene, device frames, pinned label pills, a sprite guide) rather than hard-cutting between a film and a UI demo (#5, #7, #8).
6. For proof claims, put the evidence side by side: synchronised split screens or before → after pairs with tiny labels (#15, #17).
7. Treat AI clips as scrubbable assets. Render a turntable or orbit clip and drive its frame from the code timeline (#22 does it with the cursor), so the footage stays in sync with type and the render stays deterministic.
8. Skip AI footage when the product UI is the hero. The UI-led films here (#3, #10, #17, #23) carry 28-75s on rebuilt UI alone, with 0-2 cuts. Use AI plates for openers, metaphors and end cards, not for product proof.
