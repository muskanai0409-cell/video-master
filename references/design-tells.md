# Design tells: what reads as "AI-made", and the supermemory answer

This applies to every designed frame, card, cover or graphic, moving or still. It comes from her notes #94 to #97 (lessons.md) and the "30 reasons your site looks vibecoded" study she asked for on Oct 9. The full study is `~/personal-supermemory/reports/instagram/vibe-coded-website-learnings.md`, based on @aj.on.ai's reel (2.88M plays): https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcEJDHBTyPY/

## Why it happens

AI fills every gap with the most common choice it has seen. Give the same model to ten people and you get ten sites; without a brief, "the AI fills in the blanks with the most average solution it knows" (@dmitriyinin's reel). One default is fine. Ten together read as "made by a prompt".

## The 30 tells, grouped (paraphrased)

| Group | Tells | The general fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour** | harsh gradients, rainbow colouring, purple on black, neon, basic pastels, pure white ground | One brand colour and one accent; every colour has a job; a tinted or real brand surface |
| **Type** | Inter, Geist or Space Grotesk with default sizes | Set sizes and spacing on purpose; pair a characterful face with a plain one |
| **Decoration** | Lucide icons, emojis, sparkle icons, bouncing arrows, radial orbs, dot grids, liquid glass, drop shadows, one soft radius everywhere, hover on everything | Fewer, custom, purposeful. Motion only where it explains |
| **Layout** | three feature cards in a row, all-bento pages, a fake terminal, coloured left stripes, checkmark bullets, three pricing tiers | Show one feature deeply with the real product; vary the rhythm |
| **Copy** | em dashes, "it's not X, it's Y" | Commas and full stops; the plain claim with a number |
| **Trust** | fake testimonials, no real product demo, missing loaders and legal pages | Real quotes, a real product shot |

## Where supermemory's brand book overrides the list (on purpose)

Several tells are defaults when used by accident. They are the brand when used by the book:

| Tell | supermemory's rule |
|---|---|
| **Geist** | It is the brand's secondary type, chosen in the book (Space Grotesk is the book's primary). Use the book's type scale and lowercase "supermemory", never template sizes |
| **Dot grids** | The book's imagery system, a memory field: a quiet lattice of stored squares, subjects recalled as 45° diamonds, Bayer dither (pages 30 and 31). Never decorative filler behind a hero |
| **Terminal windows** | Only real commands from the docs (`npx supermemory local`, `docker compose up -d`), typed with real output. Never a fake terminal |
| **Gradients** | Only the book's three gradients (ink to blue, blue to pale, white to sky) or Bayer-dithered light. Never a harsh rainbow |
| **Blue** | Blue 600 #0100F5 is the field; blue 500 #0562EF is the interactive accent. The weather stops (sky, dawn, dream, mist, glow, gold) live only inside images, never as text or surfaces |

## Her notes on static graphics

- **"this looks very ai generated, can you make something which is supermemory branded"** (#95, a LinkedIn graphic). A generic gradient and icon card fails. Use the film system on stills: the blue field, the dot lattice, Geist, the hairline grid and lowercase copy.
- **"does it go well and blend with other blog images we have?" then "make it more creative and cool"** (#94, a blog cover). Fit the set first, then be the most inventive one in it.
- **Viral dot graphics** (#97):
  - reference images are for the feeling, never a layout to remix;
  - research the idea, then invent the form;
  - motivational graphics stay universal: no AI, tech, brand or job words, abstract shapes, one highlighted "you", readable in 2 seconds.

## Before shipping any frame

1. Write the brief: who it's for, the one line, 3 adjectives, the brand tokens, 2 to 3 references, and a "never" list.
2. Check the frame against the 30 tells. For each one present, either it is the brand book on purpose (the table above) or it goes.
3. Look at it at phone size (390px wide).
