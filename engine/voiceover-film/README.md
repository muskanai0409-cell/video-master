## Voice timing ("the latency should be perfect")

Her notes: "make it super crisp polished and smooth there should be no latency", then "i meant the latency should be
perfect". So: natural, measured pauses, and the picture landing exactly on each word. A research run (3 lanes, 89
sourced findings, 2026-10-14) set the numbers:

| Item | Spec (research) | Used |
|---|---|---|
| Comma, where the read pauses | 200-500 ms; about half of commas get no pause | 200-300 ms; run-through commas left alone unless they are separate steps |
| List commas | may be silent | 200 ms (the audible floor) |
| Sentence stop inside a line | 500-750 ms | 500 ms (650 ms before "Again.", 550 ms after "Open Code." for the copy move) |
| Question | same as a sentence | 550 ms |
| Line gap inside a scene | 600-750 ms | 600 ms |
| Line gap at a scene change | 833-1400 ms | 800 ms (tight end of the range, for pace) |
| Before the payoff line | 1000-1400 ms | 1000 ms |
| Picture vs word | land 2-3 frames before the audible onset; never sound first | the frame at or before the audible onset, minus 2 frames |
| Click sound vs its visual | sharp onset 33-50 ms after the contact frame | 2 frames (33 ms) after |

Main sources: ITU-R BT.1359 (detect +45 / -125 ms), EBU R37 (5 ms early / 15 ms late per stage), Heide and Maempel 2010
(105 listeners, perceived sync at 39-45 ms of audio lag, 0-80 ms lag recommended, no lead), Conrey and Pisoni 2006,
Vatakis and Spence 2006, Liu et al. and Strangert on pause lengths, Cardoen (pauses over 1.4 s disrupt).

How it's done:
- **Exact timings.** The Kokoro model computes per-phoneme durations but doesn't output them. `tools/kokoro/expose_duration.py`
  copies the graph with that tensor exposed. The audio is identical to the take she approved.
- **Pause conform.** Each pause is measured as the quiet run around its punctuation (-40 dB under the line's loudest
  frame) and cut or padded in its middle, never into sound, with 6 ms fades.
- **Audible onsets.** The picture keys to when each word is heard on the polished 48 kHz track, not to the phoneme
  clock. After a pause that is the first frame above the quiet line. Inside connected speech it is the strongest
  spectral rise near the phoneme start. These differ from the phoneme clock by -88 to +69 ms (median -19). For
  example "Cursor" is heard 52 ms earlier, and the "P" of pnpm 37 ms later.
- **Ducking.** The bed is keyed from the voice with an 80 ms look-ahead, so it is already down on the first syllable.
  It holds 300 ms across her pauses and releases over 450 ms between lines. The bed sits 19 LU under her voice while she
  speaks and 12 LU under it in the gaps.
- **Music grid.** 15 bars at 91.4 BPM between the console opening and "New session", so both land on downbeats.

**Sync test through the delivery encode** (one-frame flash, click 2 frames later, same x264 + AAC 256k mux):

| Decode | Sound after picture |
|---|---|
| Edit list honoured (ffmpeg, browsers, QuickTime) | +33.3 ms |
| Edit list ignored | +54.7 ms |

Both are inside the perceived-sync window (0-75 ms of audio lag), and the sound never leads.

## QA (v1, decoded, 2026-10-14)

- 4K 95 MB, 1080p 40 MB (a lanczos cut of the 4K). Both 60fps, 57.07s, bt709, AAC 48 kHz 256k.
- Audio: -14.0 LUFS integrated, -1.5 dB peak. The bed sits 19 LU under the voice while she speaks.
- Motion: 51.6% moving. The holds sit on spoken lines: "it asks before changing code", the thesis, the chip.
- Pop check: 2 frames flagged, at 3.250s and 9.967s. These are the planned background scene cuts (the sessions and the
  console opening).
- End still: not bit-identical. The q=1 zone on the dithered film plate makes frames alternate by about 1 level.
  The fix for the next render is an IDR at q=1, then P-frames at a high QP so they decode as exact skips.
- Sync through the delivery encode: +33.3 ms (edit list honoured), +54.7 ms (ignored).
